From Selling Trucks
to Selling Certainty

How AI will reprice Indian road freight, and what OEMs, fleets and financiers should do about it.

By Hrishikesh Kumar, Dyaus AdvisoryNovember 2026

Over the next five years, AI's largest payoff in Indian road freight will not come from driverless trucks. It will come from turning operating uncertainty into a price someone is willing to guarantee.

Trucking has always been a business of educated guesses: how long a trip will take, what it will cost, whether the truck will make it. Diesel hid the cost of those guesses behind a dense refuelling network. Electric trucks remove that cushion. A loaded e-truck on a ghat road can lose a fifth of its range to terrain and weather alone, and fleet owners respond the rational way: they keep buying diesel.

We built Dyaus RangeSure, a working AI prototype, to test a simple claim. If an operator can predict cost-per-km within a narrow, honest band, it can commit to that number contractually. The company that can make that commitment changes from selling vehicles to selling certainty.

01An industry at an inflection point

India's freight system is large, road-dependent and fragmented, and it is being asked to electrify. The bottleneck is not the battery. It is trust in the number.

Policy is pushing hard: PM E-DRIVE set aside ₹500 crore in July 2025 to support 5,643 e-trucks, with incentives tied to scrapping old vehicles (EVreporter). But subsidies lower the purchase price. They do nothing about the question a fleet owner actually asks: will this truck finish my route, and what will each kilometre cost? A brochure range is measured on flat roads in mild weather. Indian freight runs through ghats, 45°C summers and overloaded tail-ends. Until someone answers that question with a number they will stand behind, electrification stays a pilot, not a purchasing decision.

02Where AI creates value: four layers, rising in worth

AI in transport creates value in four layers. Each rests on the one below and is worth more than it. Most of the market has stopped at the second.

Routing apps and telematics dashboards are crowded and cheap, because prediction and optimisation are now commodities. The layers above are scarce for a structural reason. An explanation is only trusted if the number underneath is honest about its own uncertainty. A guarantee is only safe to write if you hold enough history on that exact corridor to price the tail. That history sits with whoever already runs the trucks.

04What this means for each player

Once cost-per-km can be guaranteed, value moves from whoever sells the asset to whoever holds the corridor data. The cost of a guarantee falls with every trip logged.

The risks are real, and manageable. Telematics data quality varies from fleet to fleet. Drivers may trust a green light too much. Language models can sound confident about numbers they never computed. The fix is the one RangeSure builds in: every figure traceable to a model, a governance red line that falls back to manual planning, and an explicit refusal to price where data is thin.

—Conclusion: three calls from Dyaus

The e-truck transition will be won by whoever is first to put a guaranteed number on the road, not by whoever builds the best truck. We would make three calls today.

1

Sell the kilometre, not the truck.

Publish a guaranteed ₹/km rate card for your top ten corridors within twelve months, written the way a freight rate card is written: lane, load class, price, completion %. The buyer stops comparing purchase prices and starts comparing certainty.

The first company to publish one sets the reference price everyone else is measured against.
2

Pay fleets in price for their data.

No small operator holds enough trips to be underwritten alone, and 75% run fewer than five trucks. Offer them a lower guaranteed rate in exchange for their telematics: data-for-discount. Whoever pools the long tail of small fleets first builds a moat that money alone cannot copy.

On our model, 100 → 1,500 logged trips cuts the risk buffer from ₹4.30 to ₹1.17 per km.
3

Make "I don't know" a feature.

Insist that every AI number carries its confidence band, its source and a red line where it hands back to a human. Then turn that discipline into a range certificate attached to each corridor, which a lender can price a loan against and an insurer can price cover against.

In a market flooded with confident AI, the system that admits uncertainty is the one trusted with capital.
